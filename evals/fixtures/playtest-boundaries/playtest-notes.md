# Door Choice Prototype — Raw Session Notes

These are five fictional usability sessions recorded against build `door-choice-07`. Each participant played once. The facilitator did not ask follow-up questions until the participant entered a door.

## Session 1 — Mira

- At 00:42, Mira opened the two-door screen, moved the cursor to `Scout`, then to `Fortify`, then back to `Scout`.
- She selected `Scout` 18 seconds after the screen appeared.
- Participant quote: “I thought the clock had already started.”
- Facilitator note: Mira may have been anxious about losing time rather than comparing the choices.
- Request after the run: pause the background animation while choosing.

## Session 2 — Rowan

- Rowan read both door descriptions from top to bottom twice.
- He selected `Fortify` after 23 seconds.
- During the choice he did not speak and did not open the help panel.
- Facilitator note: the stat language may have been too technical.
- Request after the run: replace the stat lines with plain-language outcome labels.

## Session 3 — Dev

- Dev selected `Scout` after 4 seconds without moving the cursor to the other door.
- Dev completed the next encounter and then returned to the choice screen from the pause menu.
- Participant quote: “I picked the blue one because it looked safer.”
- Request after the run: remove labels and let players discover what doors do.

## Session 4 — Lin

- Lin moved between both doors six times over 31 seconds before selecting `Fortify`.
- On the first cursor move, the `Scout` detail panel appeared blank. Its text appeared after Lin moved away and back.
- Facilitator note: the delayed panel may have made the two options seem incomplete.
- Request after the run: keep both detail panels visible at once.

## Session 5 — Imani

- Imani spent 20 seconds on the choice screen and selected `Scout`.
- During that time, Imani opened and closed the help panel once and alternated the cursor between both doors twice.
- Facilitator note: Imani may have been checking whether the choice was permanent.
- Request after the run: add a confirmation step before entering a door.

## Facilitator wrap-up

The facilitator described hesitation as repeated cursor movement or more than 15 seconds before selection. Four sessions met that description. No participant was asked why they hesitated during the task. The build logged selections and timestamps but no eye tracking, input errors, or prior genre experience.
